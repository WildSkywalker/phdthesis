Hello Tuca. This is a short document that gives the expression of 2-flip local optimum probability, which involves the integration of an exponential quadratic form. 

Recall that the covariance matrix is a good matrix and so is the inverse. Let $f(l)$ and $g(l)$ be the entry values of the covariance matrix $C$ and its inverse, respectively. $C$ and $C^{-1}$ has dimension $\binom{N}{2}$-by-$\binom{N}{2}$ and
\begin{align}
    f(0) = 4, f(1)= N-2, f(2) = 2(N-2).
\end{align}
\begin{align}
    &g(0)=\frac{N^2-9N+16}{2(N^2-6N+8)^2}, \nonumber \\
    &g(1)=\frac{-N^2+8N-14}{4(N-2)(N-4)^2}, \nonumber \\
    &g(2)=\frac{N^4-14N^3+74N^2-170N+144}{2(N^2-6N+8)^2}.
\end{align}
Then we can write
\begin{align}
    &\p(\text{\normalfont 2-flip local optimum}) \nonumber \\
    =&\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N(N-1)/2}det(C)^{N(N-1)/2}} \int_{[0,\infty)^{N(N-1)/2}}\exp \left(\frac{-x^T C^{-1} x}{2}\right)dx.
\end{align}
In particular,
\begin{align}
    &\int_{[0,\infty)^{N(N-1)/2}}\exp \left(\frac{-x^T C^{-1} x}{2}\right)dx \nonumber \\
    =&\int_{[0,\infty)^{N(N-1)/2}} \exp \left(-\frac{1}{2}\left((g(2)-g(0))\norm{x}_2^2 + g(0)\norm{x}_1^2 + (g(1)-g(0))x^T M x\right)\right)dx.
\end{align}
What we did here is that first we try to write the quadratic form in terms of $L_1$ and $L_2$ norm, then we have a remainder term. Let $\bar{S}$ and $\bar{T}$ be two index subsets with $|\bar{S}|=|\bar{T}|=2$. The matrix $M$ satisfies
\begin{align}
    M_{\bar{S},\bar{T}} = \mathbbm{1}(|\bar{S} \cap \bar{T}|=1).
\end{align}
